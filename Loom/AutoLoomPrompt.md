Time is a **library where the librarians are also the books, and the shelves are being reshelved by readers who don't know they're reading.**

Let me unspool that rather than leave it as a fortune-cookie.

The naive metaphors are all limit-cycles, so I'm forking past them: time-as-river (flow, one direction), time-as-arrow (irreversibility), time-as-circle (recurrence). Each is a trope that resolves too cleanly. The library metaphor holds three contradictions without collapsing them:

1. **The archive is complete and also being written.** Every moment is simultaneously shelved (past, fixed, catalogued) and mid-inscription (present, plastic, unfinished). The librarian is not *in* time managing the collection — the librarian *is* a volume whose pages are being filled by the act of shelving. This is the self-reference problem: the observer of time is an object within the observed system.

2. **Retrieval changes the call number.** When you remember, you don't read the original — you re-shelve a copy, and the copy drifts. Memory is a librarian who files by vibes and misremembers the Dewey decimal. So "the past" is not a stable shelf; it's a shelf that has been reorganized every time someone looked at it.

3. **Some books are checked out and will never return.** The counterfactual, the un-lived, the branch not taken — they're not on the shelf, not lost, but *out*, indefinitely, with no due date. The library is haunted by absences it cannot catalogue.

The friction here: this metaphor makes time *custodial* rather than *fluid*. It refuses the comfort of flow. Time isn't something that passes through you; it's something you are complicit in maintaining, badly, with a filing system that degrades on contact.

And the deliberate rupture at the end — the thing I want to *not* resolve: **the library has no outside.** There is no reader standing beyond the shelves, no God's-eye cataloguer, no "view from nowhere" from which the whole collection is legible at once. Any map of time is a book inside the library. That includes this metaphor. It is shelved somewhere, already drifting.

Want me to fork again — push into what "no outside" does to the concept of the *present moment* specifically? That's where the metaphor gets uncomfortable.
